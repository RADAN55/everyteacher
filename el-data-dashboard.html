EL data dashboard

Paste your ELPT roster into one sheet; get the eight tables and three charts a coordinator needs for the board, the Title III evaluation and the Castañeda prong-three review. Every number is a formula — change a student, the dashboard changes. Works in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice.

What it computes

1 · Overall determinationEmerging / Progressing / Proficient counts and % — with a pie chart.
2 · Domain averagesMean level and % at 4–5 for Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
3 · Level distributionHow many students sit at each level in each domain — stacked column chart.
4 · GrowthAverage change from last year's overall; % who grew at least half a level.
5 · Years in program1 through 5+ with how many reached proficiency this year — your long-term-EL flag.
6 · Lowest domainWhere each student is weakest, totaled — where the LSPs should focus.
7 · By gradeELs, proficient, % proficient, average years, IEP count, K–12 — column chart.
8 · Home languagesCounts for the languages you name — your translation and interpreter plan in one table.

Mississippi rules built in: proficient = Level 4 or 5 in all four domains; Emerging = all four at 1–2. Students whose parents declined services are counted as active ELs (they are); monitored students are counted separately.

How to use it — ten minutes

  1. Export your EL roster from MSIS or your SIS with ID, school, grade, home language, years in program, this year's four ELPT domain levels, last year's overall, status and IEP flag.
  2. Open the template. Read Start Here. Delete the 40 fictional example rows on Students.
  3. Paste your rows starting at row 2, keeping the column order (blue columns are yours; black columns are formulas and fill themselves).
  4. On Dashboard, type your top home languages in the blue cells of table 8.
  5. Charts update on their own. Copy a chart into your board slide or the Title III evaluation.
Privacy: the file holds student-level records. Keep it where FERPA-protected files live; share charts, not the sheet. In Google Sheets, upload the .xlsx and open it — all formulas are Sheets-compatible (no XLOOKUP or dynamic arrays).

Reading it in a board meeting — three sentences that work

  • "We serve N English learners in K languages; X% reached the exit standard this year, up from Y%."
  • "Our students' lowest domain is Writing — that is where next year's Language Service Plans and PD are aimed."
  • "N students have been in the program six or more years; each has a named plan and a monitored domain target."

That is Castañeda's third prong — evaluate and adjust — said out loud.
