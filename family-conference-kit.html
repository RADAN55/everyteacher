Before the conference

The interpreter checklist

  • Book it a week ahead through the office (request form). Phone or video is fine; a trained interpreter, not the student, a sibling or a classmate.
  • Seat a triangle: you face the parent, the interpreter to the side. Speak to the parent — "your child," not "tell her that her child."
  • Short chunks, one idea at a time, then pause. No idioms ("on the ball"), no acronyms without the words.
  • Check understanding by asking the parent to tell you the plan back, not by asking "do you understand?"
  • Everything on paper goes home in Spanish — the summary this page prints, the report card explainer, your contact card.
  • Budget 20 minutes, not 10: interpreted conversation takes twice as long.

Bring

  • Two pieces of the student's work: one from the start of the term, one recent — growth is the story.
  • The ELPT levels by domain (the EL teacher has them) and what they mean in your class.
  • The Language Service Plan's accommodations, in plain words.
  • Grades, with content and language separated if you grade that way.
  • One thing the family can do at home in Spanish.
  • Your phone number or email on a card, in Spanish.

The invitation (call, text or note)

Hello, this is ___ from ___ High School, your child ___'s ___ teacher. I would like to meet with you about how ___ is doing — good news and plans. We can meet on ___ at ___, in person or by phone, with a Spanish interpreter at no cost. Please reply or call ___.
Hola, le habla ___ de la escuela ___, maestro/a de ___ de su hijo/a ___. Me gustaría reunirme con usted para hablar de cómo va ___: buenas noticias y planes. Podemos reunirnos el ___ a las ___, en persona o por teléfono, con intérprete de español sin costo. Por favor, responda o llame al ___.

The fifteen-minute script

English on top, Spanish below, a speaker for each line. Read it, or let the family hear the Spanish while the interpreter listens. Fill the blanks from your notes before the meeting.

1 · Welcome
Thank you for coming. I am ___, your child's ___ teacher. This is ___, our interpreter; everything we say is private.
Gracias por venir. Soy ___, maestro/a de ___ de su hijo/a. Él/ella es ___, nuestro/a intérprete; todo lo que decimos es confidencial.
2 · Strengths first
I want to start with what your child does well. ___ is ___, ___ and ___. I see it every day.
Quiero empezar con lo que su hijo/a hace bien. ___ es ___, ___ y ___. Lo veo todos los días.
3 · The English test, in one breath
Every spring your child takes a test of English in four parts: listening, speaking, reading and writing. Each part gets a level from 1 to 5. Your child's levels are ___. Your child finishes the program when every part is a 4 or 5.
Cada primavera su hijo/a toma un examen de inglés en cuatro partes: escuchar, hablar, leer y escribir. Cada parte recibe un nivel del 1 al 5. Los niveles de su hijo/a son ___. Su hijo/a termina el programa cuando cada parte está en 4 o 5.
4 · What it means in my class
In my class that means your child can ___, and we are working on ___. The grade shows what your child knows about ___, not how much English they have.
En mi clase eso significa que su hijo/a puede ___, y estamos trabajando en ___. La calificación muestra lo que su hijo/a sabe de ___, no cuánto inglés tiene.
5 · The plan
Your child has a language plan. It gives extra time, ___ and ___. These are not favors; they are the plan.
Su hijo/a tiene un plan de idioma. Le da tiempo adicional, ___ y ___. No son favores; son el plan.
6 · At home, in your language
The best help at home is in your language: ask what they learned today, have them explain it to you, read together in Spanish, talk about the news. Strong Spanish makes strong English.
La mejor ayuda en casa es en su idioma: pregúntele qué aprendió hoy, pídale que se lo explique, lean juntos en español, hablen de las noticias. Un español fuerte hace un inglés fuerte.
7 · Your questions
What questions do you have? What do you want me to know about your child?
¿Qué preguntas tiene? ¿Qué quiere que yo sepa de su hijo/a?
8 · Close
Here is my phone number and the best way to reach me. I will send a note in Spanish in ___ weeks. Thank you.
Aquí está mi número de teléfono y la mejor manera de comunicarse conmigo. Le enviaré una nota en español en ___ semanas. Gracias.
The one sentence that changes the meeting: "The grade shows what your child knows about biology, not how much English they have." Families of English learners have often been told, for years, that their child is behind. Say what the child knows first.

Three things to say about an English learner's grades

  • A content grade is about content. In biology, history and math the grade measures what your child knows about the subject; the plan says language errors are not counted against them there.
  • Levels and grades are different. A Level 2 in English can earn an A in Algebra. The English levels come from the spring test, not from the report card.
  • Missing work is the usual reason for a low grade, not language — and an Incomplete can still be fixed. Ask what is missing and by when.

For the family: six questions to ask

Print this card for the family's side of the table — or send it home before the conference so they arrive with questions. Any family may ask for a conference, with an interpreter, at any time, free.

  1. What is my child good at in your class?
    ¿En qué es bueno/a mi hijo/a en su clase?
  2. What are my child's English levels, and which one do you want to grow this year?
    ¿Cuáles son los niveles de inglés de mi hijo/a y cuál quiere que suba este año?
  3. What help does my child get in your class and on tests?
    ¿Qué ayuda recibe mi hijo/a en su clase y en los exámenes?
  4. Is my child on track to graduate? What is missing?
    ¿Va mi hijo/a por buen camino para graduarse? ¿Qué falta?
  5. What can I do at home, in Spanish?
    ¿Qué puedo hacer en casa, en español?
  6. How do I reach you, and will you send notes in Spanish?
    ¿Cómo me comunico con usted y me enviará notas en español?

If you do not understand · Si no entiende

I don't understand. Can you say it again, more slowly? Can you write it down?
No entiendo. ¿Puede repetirlo más despacio? ¿Puede escribirlo?
I would like an interpreter for this meeting.
Quisiera un intérprete para esta reunión.
Please send papers home in Spanish.
Por favor, envíe los documentos a casa en español.
