Before day 1 the afternoon you hear a newcomer is coming (or the first 30 minutes if you didn't)

Find out four things

  • Home language and whether the student reads and writes it (ask the registrar for the Home Language Survey; ask the family, not the student).
  • Prior schooling — last grade completed, any gap years. A 16-year-old with grade 9 completed is a different week than a 16-year-old with grade 5.
  • Who is at home and the best phone number and language for texts.
  • Anything the family wants you to know — health, fears, a sibling in the building.

Set the room

  • Seat near you, near the board, beside a bilingual peer if there is one. Name tent in both scripts.
  • Print the First 100 Words flashcards for "People" and "Places" and tape six of them where they belong: office, restroom, cafeteria, gym, library, your door.
  • Charge a Chromebook; log it in; open the Newcomer Guide in the family's language and leave the tab open.
  • Choose a buddy — a kind, patient student, bilingual if possible, same lunch. Brief them (checklist on the right).

Tell the building

One email to every teacher of record, the counselor, the nurse, the bus driver and the cafeteria manager: name, pronunciation, home language, level (new arrival = assume Level 1), the buddy's name, and "please do not use the student as an interpreter for the family." Attach the EL Student Profile once you have it.

Buddy checklist · brief in 5 minutes

Day 1 survive the building · belong to one adult

First 20 minutes — the walkthrough

Script (slow, with gestures; do it in the home language if you can):
"Hi, I'm Mr. Daniel. Welcome. [hand on chest] Mr. Daniel. [point] You? … Good. Come. [walk] This is the office. If you are sick, if you are lost — office. [walk] Restroom. Boys. Girls. You need it? Show me this card [hall pass]. [walk] Cafeteria. Lunch, 11:30. [hold up tray card] Tray. Your number is ____. [walk] Gym. [walk] Library — quiet. [walk] This is your locker, 318. Watch: 12 — 4 — 30. You try. Again. Good. [walk to your room] This is my room, 118. Any problem, any time — Room 118. Say it: one-one-eight."

Rest of the day

  • Give the schedule with each room number written large; walk it once with the buddy.
  • In your class: the student does not have to speak today. A labeled picture task, the first 10 words with the tap-to-hear page, headphones on.
  • Three phrases on an index card, both languages: Can you say that again, please? · Can you show me? · I need help.
  • Before dismissal: bus number on the hand or phone; walk to the bus loop; find the driver by name.

The family call (after school)

Interpreter on the line if you don't speak the language. Keep it to four minutes:
"This is ____, ____'s English teacher. Today went well. ____ found the lunchroom, the bus, and my room. Two things: please bring any old report cards or school papers, even photos on your phone, by Friday — they can earn credits. And the school will send home a letter about English support; it is support, not a problem. Best number for texts? Best language? … Any question for me? Thank you — ____ did well."
Use today Newcomer Guide — chapter 3 & 4 First 100 Words — People, Places

Do not screen today. A student who just arrived scores as a student who just arrived.

Day 2 names, routines, the first fifty words

Your class

  • Greeting routine the student can own by Friday: "Good morning, Mr. Daniel." — "Good morning, ____." Same words every day.
  • First 100 Words: Backpack and Classroom groups. Tap-to-hear, then a real-object match: hold up the pencil, student finds the card.
  • Teach the ✓ "known" button — the first thing the student controls on the Chromebook.
  • Model asking: you ask the buddy "Can you show me?"; the buddy shows; the student copies. Twice.

Paperwork, quietly

  • Confirm the registrar has the Home Language Survey filed and the screener scheduled (30 days from start of year; 10 school days mid-year).
  • Start the EL Student Profile with what you know; leave levels blank until the screener.
  • Ask the counselor to pull or request records; note what the family said they'll bring.
Watch for: eating alone, long bathroom trips, head down all period. Each is normal in week one; each is also a reason to check in, not to wait.
Use today First 100 Words — Backpack, Classroom Student Profile (draft)

Day 3 the schedule words · the first written English

Your class

  • First 100 Words: Schedule & time and Teacher instructions. Act out the instruction words — listen, write, raise your hand, line up — as a game, whole class, newcomer included.
  • First writing: the student copies name, date, teacher, room number, and the three phrases. This is Level 1 writing and it counts.
  • Quiz mode on the first 40 words — "hide Spanish." Celebrate the number, not the misses.

With the content teachers

  • Hand each one a copy of the accommodations cards for Visuals, Home-language support and Extra wait time — the three that cost nothing and change everything in week one.
  • Ask each for one thing the student can do in their room this week: label a diagram, copy a timeline, solve the numbers-only problems.
Use today First 100 Words — Schedule, Instructions Accommodations cards (3 for content teachers) Frames — Classroom talk, Level 1

Day 4 lunch, rules, feelings — the words that keep a kid safe

Your class

  • First 100 Words: Lunch, Rules & behavior, Feelings & asking for help. The feelings group is the most important ten words of the week: help, I don't understand, slowly, sick, nervous.
  • Read Newcomer Guide chapter 7 (Classroom culture) together in the home language — the unwritten rules nobody tells a newcomer: raising a hand, personal space, "on time means before the bell."
  • Phone rule, dress code and the hall pass, demonstrated, not explained.

Screener, if it's scheduled

  • Walk to the testing room the day before. Headphones tried on. "It only decides what help you get" — say it in the home language.
  • After the screener: levels into the Profile, then the LSP Builder drafts the Language Service Plan from them. Lane, services and accommodations pre-check; edit; print for signatures.
Use today First 100 Words — Lunch, Rules, Help Newcomer Guide — chapter 7 LSP Builder (after screener)

Day 5 belonging · the first self-assessment · the letter home

Your class

  • Last group: Grades & work. Then "Hear all 100" with the student following along — the week in four minutes.
  • Can-Do Checklist, Level 1 rows only, in the home language. The student checks what is true; you check with them. Most will have 6–10 boxes. Save it — this is the baseline you'll compare in January.
  • Join one thing: walk the student to a practice, a club, a team manager, the band room. Introduce them to the adult by name. Put it on the schedule card.

Paperwork, finished

  • Parent notification letter 1 (identification and placement), English page + home-language page, sent home or mailed — within 30 days of the start of year, 10 school days mid-year. Log the date.
  • LSP signed by you and the principal; copies to every teacher of record with the Profile.
  • Records from home arrived? Transcript evaluation request to the counselor in writing.

The second family contact

"____ finished the first week. ____ knows the building, has a buddy, and learned about 100 school words. Next week we start reading and writing. The letter in your bag explains the English program — I'd like to meet you, with an interpreter, any afternoon next week. Which day?"

Day-5 exit ticket · did the week work?

Ask the student to do these five things. Four of five means the week worked. Fewer means repeat days 1–2 next week with the buddy, not more worksheets.

1 · Say it"Good morning, Mr. ____." — and your name back when you greet them.
2 · Find itWalk you to the restroom, the cafeteria and their locker without pointing from you; open the locker.
3 · Ask itUse one of the three phrases without being prompted — "Can you show me?" counts.
4 · Show itOpen the Chromebook, open the 100 Words page, and show you the ✓ count (any number above 20).
5 · Name one personA classmate, a coach, a librarian — one person in the building who is not you.
Teacher's own exit ticketProfile, LSP and letter 1 exist and are dated; every teacher of record has the Profile; the family has your number. If any is missing, that is Monday's first task.
