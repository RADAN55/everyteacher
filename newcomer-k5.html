K–2: the first ten days

A five- or six-year-old newcomer is not behind. They are learning to read the room before they read the page. The job of the first two weeks is safety, routine, and a hundred words they can use.

Set up the room

Before they walk in

  • Picture schedule card set (in Printables).
  • Labels with pictures on door, sink, cubbies, materials.
  • A visual “I need…” card on the desk: bathroom, water, help, nurse, I don't understand.
  • Buddy chosen for kindness, not language.
  • Books in the home language, even two, in the class library.
Language that works at 5–7

Say less, show more

  • Same short phrases every day: “Line up.” “Eyes on me.” “Show me.” Gesture every time.
  • Songs and chants carry grammar for free; use the ones the class already sings.
  • Total physical response: say it, do it, have them do it.
  • Accept one word or a point as an answer for weeks. Then ask for two words.
Reading and math

Start from what transfers

  • Spanish, Portuguese, Vietnamese: the alphabet transfers; teach the sounds that differ (short vowels, th, sh).
  • Arabic, Chinese: teach letter shapes with sand, body movement, tracing.
  • Math: numbers and operations transfer almost completely; the word problem is the barrier, not the math.
  • Pair every number with the quantity (dots, cubes) for the first month.

The four lanes, for little ones

NewcomerWeeks 1–8 for mostPoints, matches, sorts, draws, copies. Says single words and memorized chunks. Needs a buddy and pictures for everything.
EmergingOften by mid-yearTwo- and three-word phrases. Follows routine directions without the gesture. Starts to retell with picture support.
ProgressingYear two for manySimple sentences about what they see and do. Reads decodable text. Needs vocabulary pre-taught for content.
ChallengeNear grade-level peersPush for complete sentences, past tense, “because.” Give them the job of explaining to the next newcomer.

Grades 3–5: the first ten days

A nine-year-old newcomer is old enough to be embarrassed and young enough to catch up fast. They often read and compute at or above grade level in their first language. Protect their dignity and give them content from day one.

Dignity

Don't send them to kindergarten

  • No kindergarten books in front of peers. Use high-interest, low-text books, graphic novels, and bilingual texts.
  • Group them with strong, kind readers, not with the lowest group.
  • Let them show what they know in the home language on paper; you can get it translated.
Content from day one

Lower the language, not the thinking

  • Every lesson: five words with pictures, one sentence frame, one diagram.
  • Use EL Bridge with the K–12 ELA, math, or science standard; the Newcomer lane is written for exactly this.
  • Accept labeled drawings and single-sentence answers as evidence for a month.
Reading transfer

Readers in one language become readers in two

  • Find out if they read in the home language. If yes, comprehension strategies transfer; teach English sounds and vocabulary.
  • If no, this is a reading-instruction need, not an English need; loop in the reading interventionist.
  • Cognates for Spanish speakers: 30–40% of academic English. Make a cognate wall.

The four lanes at 8–11

NewcomerSemester one for mostLabels, matches, copies sentence frames. Math computation at grade level with the problem read aloud.
EmergingOften by springShort sentences with a frame; reads picture-supported text; retells with a sequence organizer.
ProgressingYear twoParagraphs with a frame that fades; grade-level text with glossed words; participates in group work with a role.
ChallengeNear exitGrade-level tasks; coach academic vocabulary and connectors. Give them a leadership role in groups.

First 20 phrases · Primeras 20 frases

Family first-contact script · Guion para la primera llamada

English

Hello, this is ___, your child's teacher at ___ School. I'm calling to say welcome. ___ had a good first day. Today ___ learned where things are and sat with a friend named ___. Can you tell me: What language do you speak at home? What did ___ study last year? Is there anything ___ needs from us? You can call me at ___ anytime; we will find an interpreter. Thank you.

Español

Hola, habla ___, maestro/a de su hijo/a en la escuela ___. Llamo para darles la bienvenida. ___ tuvo un buen primer día. Hoy ___ aprendió dónde están las cosas y se sentó con un amigo/a que se llama ___. ¿Me puede decir: qué idioma hablan en casa? ¿Qué estudió ___ el año pasado? ¿Hay algo que ___ necesite de nosotros? Puede llamarme al ___ cuando quiera; buscaremos un intérprete. Gracias.
