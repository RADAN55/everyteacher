A newcomer arrives in October

Tenth grade. Spanish-speaking. Four years of schooling in Honduras, then two years of none. Enrolled by an aunt on a Tuesday, in class on Wednesday. Ninety days, in order.

What a leader should notice

Every step above is a compliance item (HLS, screening, notification, LSP, accommodations, access to core content) done as a kindness instead of as paperwork. The audit and the welcome are the same checklist.

What it cost

Interpreter line: about $40 for the month. A dictionary: $12. Teacher time: one 15-minute meeting per content teacher. Everything else was already built.

Where districts usually fail

Day 1 (asking for papers), Day 3 (missing the notification clock), and Week 2 (handing a Level 1 student a grade-level worksheet in English and calling it inclusion).

The student who never exits

Eighth grade, in U.S. schools since kindergarten, ELPT 4-4-4-3 three springs in a row. Fluent in the hallway, stuck on the writing domain. A long-term EL by every definition.

What a leader should notice

Long-term ELs are a program-design problem, not a student problem. The fix was diagnostic (which domain), specific (one goal), and time-bound (the window). It cost one hour of scheduling.

Why it matters to the district

Every year a student like this stays identified, the district carries the cost and the accountability weight, and the student carries a label that limits scheduling. The exit standard is strict; the response has to be precise.

The number to track

How many students are one domain short of exit. The Outcomes Dashboard produces that list in a minute; it should be on the fall data meeting agenda every year.

A district gets the monitoring letter

Two thousand students, about a hundred ELs, one part-time EL teacher, no coordinator. A state monitoring visit is scheduled in six weeks. The superintendent asks: where are we?

What a leader should notice

Nothing here needed a consultant or a purchase. It needed someone who knew the checklist and had six weeks. The tools on this hub are that checklist.

The hidden finding

The two re-entry students in week 3. Monitoring is a compliance item on paper; in practice it is how a district catches a former EL who is failing ninth grade quietly.

Why a coordinator

Every gap above existed because the work belonged to no one. A half-time coordinator costs less than one finding and is the single structural fix that keeps the audit score where it landed.
