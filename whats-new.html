NewEvaluate Your Own EL Program — the consultant's method, for free

A six-session course that teaches a district team to run its own EL program evaluation instead of buying one. Session one refuses to start until the team writes the decision the evaluation has to inform. Session two carries the data request to send the data office as written, and the warning that one row per student per year is the thing districts get wrong. Sessions three to five work the analysis: identification rate against home-language surveys, enrollment-to-screening and screening-to-service timelines, long-term EL count, progress against attainment, the years-in-program table that shows where growth stalls, and the current-EL / former-EL / never-EL comparison that is the best free indicator of program quality anyone has. Session six is five whys, triangulation, and the one-page board memo with 30/90/180-day actions and named owners. Each session carries reading thresholds, red flags, a worked composite example and a deliverable that saves as you type; the six assemble into one printable findings report. It is deliberately not the Self-Audit — that answers whether you are compliant, this answers whether the program works, and a district can pass one while failing the other.

NewSheltered — 12 minutes as an English learner

Sheltered is a simulation for general-education teachers and administrators. You take a real content lesson — Biology on osmosis in Spanish, Algebra I on two-step equations in Vietnamese, or U.S. History on the Dust Bowl in Arabic — with two minutes to study and five questions in 100 seconds, and no support of any kind. Then you take it again: same lesson, same language, same clock, parallel questions of the same difficulty, with six supports added — slowed sentence-by-sentence audio, a visual that carries the content, a six-word glossary, cognates and signal words highlighted, a word bank on each question, and a sentence frame. Extended time is deliberately withheld so the only variable is the scaffolding. The debrief puts both scores side by side, names each support with the lane it serves, reveals what the lesson actually said, and carries a script for running the whole thing with a faculty in twenty-five minutes. Nothing leaves the device; the result prints as a PD record.

NewStorm Lens: Point. Name. Say.

Storm Lens turns the Chromebook camera into a vocabulary teacher: point it at anything in the room and the room becomes the word list. Two recognition models run in the browser — 80 objects live, 1,000 on Freeze & scan — mapped onto a 334-word school bank with a Word Bank to browse by category. Every word opens a card with the Spanish, a cognate flag, four-lane sentence frames, a verb collocation and example sentence, pronunciation coaching keyed to Spanish-to-English transfer, plurals, a word family, a quick check, an ELPA21-style “describe the picture” speaking task auto-scored on four points, and a write-it check — all with say-it-back speech checking that shows word by word what was heard. The student's own photos become their Word Wall, four practice modes (say, listen & find, spell, build the sentence) that start with the weakest words, twenty scavenger missions, and rolling end credits. It plays like a film: opening titles, a narrator, chapter cards, a score, theater mode. For teachers: hunt links, a class view built from student files, a printable exit ticket, a describe-the-picture rubric and a device check. Installs as an app and works offline after the first load. No account; nothing leaves the device. Filed under Interactive tools and first on the student path.

NewSentence Lab: English Grammar in Real Life

Sentence Lab is a 25-page grammar packet for grades 9–12 English learners. Sixteen features — from subject + verb to connectors, sentence repair and expansion — each set in a realistic school scene with a photo study, a one-line Grammar Lens, and a Newcomer lane and a Progressing lane side by side: same grammar goal, different language supports, English and Spanish on every page. Page 19 is a shared writing project, page 20 a before/after six-skill check, and pages 21–25 the teacher key with answers for both lanes, a four-row rubric and feedback guidance. Web page plus free PDF; filed under Bulletins & packets and on the student and content-teacher paths.

NewEnglish On Your Own — ELPA21 practice built to the real blueprint

English On Your Own is self-paced ELPA21 practice for grades 9–12, written to the published test specifications: all twenty-two task types — Listen and Match, Listen for Information, Academic Debate, Short Conversation, Academic Lecture, Interactive Student Presentation, Short Paragraphs, the literary and informational sets, Argument and Support Essay, Discrete Editing, Writing Questions, Respond to Peer Email, Storyboard, Construct a Claim, Compare Pictures, Language Arts Presentation, Analyze a Visual and a Claim, and Observe and Report. Each one opens with what the task asks and which ELP subclaim it measures. A sixty-item full practice form follows the real split (23 listening, 26 reading, 5 writing, 6 speaking) and returns a level estimate per domain plus an Emerging / Progressing / Proficient determination, a by-standard breakdown, and the weakest domain to work on next. Speaking records through the microphone and checks what was actually said; writing gets an automatic check plus the rater's checklist. Directions in six languages with right-to-left Arabic; passages and prompts stay in English, as on the test. No account, nothing sent anywhere. The level estimate uses the app's own bands and cannot reclassify a student.
