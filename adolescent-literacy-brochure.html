The five moves

The guide's main point: reading is every teacher's job. Moves 1–4 fit inside any lesson in any subject; move 5 is for students who need more than a classroom teacher can give.

  1. Teach vocabulary on purpose (Strong evidence). Teach the key words directly, then have students see, say and use them again and again; it can take up to 17 encounters to own a new word.
  2. Show students how good readers think (Strong). Name a strategy (summarize, question, paraphrase, infer, organize), model it by thinking aloud, then step back from guided practice to independent use.
  3. Let students talk about what the reading means (Moderate). Real questions, follow-ups like “Where does it say that?”, small-group roles and a routine that ends by summing up.
  4. Give students reasons to want to read (Moderate). Clear goals with specific feedback, some choice, and topics tied to students' lives, not prize contests.
  5. Get struggling readers real, extra help (Strong). Screen, diagnose and give smaller-group, more-time intervention from a trained specialist.

Every move carries a short note for teachers of English learners, a Monday-morning checklist closes the inside panel, and the back explains what the 2008 guide does and doesn't cover.
