The five levels, in one screen

ELPA21 (Mississippi calls the test the ELPT) scores each of four domains — Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing — on a 1-to-5 scale every spring. A level is a measure of English, not of intelligence, effort or content knowledge. A Level 2 student may be the strongest mathematician in the room.

1Beginning

Understands and produces a few words and set phrases with pictures, gestures and a patient partner. Everything new needs a visual.

2Early intermediate

Follows simple directions and short, familiar talk; speaks and writes in short sentences about known topics. Frames and word banks turn silence into answers.

3Intermediate

Follows a lesson on a familiar topic, reads a page-long text with support, writes a paragraph. Can look fluent in the hallway and still miss half of a lecture.

4Early advanced

Handles grade-level text and talk with vocabulary preview; writes multi-paragraph pieces with evidence. Academic register and precise grammar are what is left.

5Advanced

Reads, writes, listens and speaks about grade-level content independently. Still benefits from writing feedback like any student.

How the four numbers become one status

Emerging

Every domain is a 1 or a 2. The student is building the basics in all four skills.

Progressing

Everything in between — the most common status in a high school. Usually at least one domain is a 3 or better and at least one is still below a 4.

Proficient

Every domain is a 4 or a 5. In Mississippi this is the exit line (State Board, June 19, 2025): the student leaves EL services and is monitored for four years.

From a status to a lane

The hub's tools work in four lanes, because "Progressing" alone is too wide to plan from. This page assigns the lane like this:

Newcomer

Three or four domains at Level 1, or a student in a first year of U.S. schooling who tests Emerging. Survival English comes first; content comes through pictures, the home language and doing.

Emerging

Emerging status otherwise: 1s and 2s, with at least one 2. Every task needs a visual, a word bank and a frame — and the student can show content knowledge with them.

Progressing

Progressing status with fewer than three domains at 4 or 5. Grade-level work with scaffolds: organizers before writing, partner talk before whole-class, academic words required.

Challenge

Three or four domains at 4 or 5, or Proficient and being monitored. Grade-level expectations with a vocabulary preview; push the academic register; advocate for honors, AP and dual credit.

The lowest domain plans the task

A student who is 4 · 4 · 4 · 2 is Progressing, but the Writing 2 decides what a fair written assessment looks like. The one-student view reads each domain separately for that reason.

Levels move about one a year

Reaching Proficient typically takes four to seven years; academic reading and writing come last. A Level 3 in year three is on schedule, not behind.

Four things a roster cannot tell you — and the level does not change

"He speaks fine in the hall, so he's fluent."

Social English arrives in one to two years; the English of a textbook, a lab report or a DBQ takes five or more. A Speaking 4 with a Reading 2 is common. Trust the Reading and Writing numbers for schoolwork.

"She's been here three years — why is she still Level 2?"

Interrupted schooling, limited literacy in the home language, or a late start all slow the climb, and none of them is the student's choice. Three years at Level 2 is a reason to look at the plan, not at the student.

"If I grade her writing for grammar she'll learn faster."

On a non-ELA assignment, a grammar penalty measures English, not biology, and the student already takes a test of English every spring. Score the content; mark the language separately; teach one language feature at a time.

"A translation app on the test would be fairer."

Not on MAAP or the ELPT — the home language is not permitted on the ELPT and only a print word-to-word dictionary is allowed on MAAP, and only when it is in the plan. In class, a translation app for understanding is fine; for writing to be turned in, it is not.
