For the teacher: how to use it, transcripts and key

Four units, each with the three ELPA21 listening formats and two speaking formats. A student hears each clip at most twice at a natural pace, which is the test's own rule; the Support switch on each task allows unlimited listens, a slower speed and the transcript after the first listen, for Newcomer and Emerging students. The transcript and the reason for the answer appear after every item, so a wrong answer is a lesson, not a loss.

Speaking tasks are recorded on the student's own device with the browser's microphone and never leave it. The student listens back, ticks a three-line self-check, and then hears a model answer at the Progressing / Challenge level. Lane frames (Newcomer, Emerging, Progressing, Challenge) sit under every prompt. If the browser cannot record, the task still works with a phone voice memo and the on-screen clock.

In class

Pairs, one device, earbuds split. Listening: both listen, each chooses on paper, then compare before tapping. Speaking: A records while B listens for the self-check lines, then swap. The recordings are evidence for the LSP speaking goal when a student saves one to the class folder; the date is in the file name.

At home

One task a night, ten minutes. Families can listen along; the transcript is in English, and the pie unit's words are on the Six Southern Pies cover page in English and Spanish.
