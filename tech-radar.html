1

Khan Academy Writing Coach · an essay coach that asks, hints and flags, but never writes

Khan Academy (AI on Google Gemini since Jan 21, 2026) · rebuilt Jan 2026: Gemini models, four feedback categories, Essay Assignments Library, feedback-only mode · free for U.S. teachers and their students · browser / Chromebook · grades 7–12 · free Khan Academy student accounts, join code or link

Writing Coach walks a student through one essay in four stages — Understanding the prompt, Outlining, Drafting in its own editor, and Revising — and at each stage it asks questions and gives hints instead of producing text. Khan Academy made it free for every U.S. teacher and their students in February 2025; what is new this year is dated on two blogs. On January 21, 2026 Google announced at BETT that Writing Coach now runs on Gemini models and that a teacher can choose "a full interactive experience or a 'feedback-only' mode." Khan Academy's own post nine days later (Jan 30, 2026) lists the rest: feedback is now sorted into four categories a Level 3 student can actually act on — Thesis & Focus, Evidence & Reasoning, Structure & Organization, Language — and there is an Essay Assignments Library of ready-made persuasive, informational and literary-analysis prompts, filterable by grade 5–12 and by ELA or social studies, each with instructions you can rewrite before assigning (a word count and a sentence-frame list fit in that box). For an EL student the value is the Language category and the chat: a student can ask "how do I say this more formally?" or "is this sentence complete?" and get a hint, not a rewrite, with the whole transcript visible to you afterward. Setup: a free account at khanmigo.ai, Khanmigo menu → Writing Coach → Explore and assign; pick a library prompt or "Use my own prompt," tick full essay or feedback/revision-only, choose or create a class, and you get a join code and a URL to post in Classroom. Students need a free Khan Academy account (Google sign-in works), or they already have one if the district pays for Khanmigo. The dashboard shows each student's steps completed, time spent, word count, AI suggestions offered and resolved, originality flags ("Critical" or "Questionable," with the reason) and the chat transcript. Three cautions. Feedback and chat are in English only — there is no Spanish mode, so this is a Progressing-and-up tool unless you run it from the projector. The originality check fires when text that was not in the outline appears in the draft, which is exactly what a student who drafts in Spanish and pastes a translation will do, so say out loud: write it here, in English, badly first. And Khan Academy's help page says the first draft can be submitted for feedback once; use feedback-only mode for a second round. No COPPA statement on the Writing Coach pages themselves; Khan Academy's standard student privacy policy applies, and the district can sign its student-data agreement.

Progressing lane · 90-minute block (Chromebooks): the night before, open the Essay Assignments Library, filter grade 9 · persuasive, and pick a prompt the class already has opinions about (the dress-code or phones-in-class prompt works). In the instructions box write: "Three paragraphs, about 200 words. Use these frames: I believe that … because … / One reason is … / Some people say … but …," and post the join URL in Classroom. In class, model the Understanding stage on the projector for 10 minutes: ask the coach "explain the prompt in simple English" and "what does persuasive mean here?" Pairs talk through their position in Spanish or English for 5 minutes, then each student builds the outline alone, in English (15 min). Drafting inside the editor, no pasting, 30 minutes; you circulate with the dashboard open and go to whoever has the lowest word count. Students request feedback, read only the Language and Thesis & Focus comments, and fix three things (15 min). Exit ticket in Classroom: paste your thesis sentence and one coach comment you fixed, in your own words (10 min). Last 5 minutes: read two transcripts aloud, names off, and name one good question a student asked the coach. Challenge lane gets a literary-analysis prompt from the library, 400 words, and must ask the coach to "push back on my evidence" once. Emerging lane writes a five-sentence paragraph in a Google Doc first, then uses a feedback-only assignment to get the Language comments on it. Newcomer lane skips this one — the interface is all English text — and does the same prompt as a Snorkl whiteboard (#3) with a sentence frame.

Guided Learning in Google Classroom · an AI tutor attached to your own assignment

Google · new Nov 3–4, 2026 · free with every Workspace for Education edition (Fundamentals, Standard, Plus) · browser / Chromebook · all Gemini-supported languages · admin must have Gemini on for students

Since November 4, 2026 a teacher can attach a Guided Learning activity to any Classroom assignment the same way you attach a Doc. You pick one to five sources from your class materials (a Doc, Slides or PDF — Google recommends one), and the student gets a Gemini tutor that knows only that material and is built, in Google's words, "to help you learn, not to give you direct answers": it asks guiding questions, breaks the task into steps and offers hints. The student opens it from Classwork in a Focus Mode tab with the Gemini side panel hidden and new chats disabled, so the conversation stays on your text. Because it runs in every Gemini-supported language, a Spanish-speaking student can ask "explícame el párrafo 2 en inglés sencillo" and get the explanation in English, or ask in English and switch to Spanish when stuck. Unlike the open Gemini study tools (#7 below), you see this one: an Insights panel shows who opened it, a class summary of common questions and "areas to support," and each student's full transcript — a quick way to find which paragraph or word tripped the group before the writing starts. Nothing to install; it is included in Education Fundamentals, so Laurel's domain has it as soon as the rollout reaches you (extended rollout began Nov 3). Your admin must have the Gemini app turned on for both the teacher and the student OU; the student-side Gemini in Classroom opened to all ages in August 2026, but Google said on Nov 3 that K–12 student access to Gemini will switch to off-until-the-admin-opts-in "in the coming months," so confirm with your district before building a unit on it. Google's own caveats: AI insights "are not intended for assessment or placement," students can delete a chat (which removes it from your transcript view, if your admin allows deletion), and insight summaries have a usage cap. Preview as a student before assigning — it takes one click.

Progressing lane · 90-minute block (Chromebooks): the night before, attach one source to a Classroom assignment — a 400-word Level 3 science or history text with the six text-dependent questions at the bottom of the same Doc — and name the Guided Learning activity "Read with a tutor." Model on the projector for 10 minutes: ask it "What is paragraph 1 about, in simple English?", then deliberately give a wrong answer to question 1 and show how it hints instead of correcting you; show the Spanish switch once. Students read the text twice and work through questions 1–3 with the tutor, rule: ask in English first, Spanish only after two tries (25 min). They close the tab and answer questions 4–6 in the assignment Doc in their own words, tutor off (20 min). Pairs compare answers and record one 60-second explanation of question 5 in Snorkl or on a phone (15 min). While they talk, open Insights, read the class summary, and reteach the one word or sentence that shows up most (20 min). Newcomer lane uses a 150-word text, asks the tutor in Spanish, then types each answer in English from a sentence frame. Challenge lane gets a Level 4 text and the instruction "ask the tutor to challenge your claim," then writes a claim-evidence paragraph.

3

Snorkl · students explain out loud on a whiteboard, AI feedback in English or Spanish

Snorkl · Audio Clips Feb 2026 · video prompts summer 2026 · Reading Fluency subject Aug 2026 · free Teacher plan (unlimited classes, 20 activities) · browser / Chromebook with a mic · Google or Microsoft sign-in, or guest link · iKeepSafe COPPA/FERPA, Common Sense Privacy seal

Snorkl is a web whiteboard where a student answers a prompt by talking, drawing and typing while the screen records, then gets two or three AI comments on the explanation (a strength, something to make clearer, a nudge to say more) and a 0–4 explanation score the teacher can hide. The pieces that make it an EL tool arrived this year, each dated in Snorkl's own changelog: in February 2026 you can record the question in your own voice (Audio Clips, which Snorkl says it built "especially for language learning"); over the summer you can embed a YouTube clip or upload a video for students to watch and respond to, add audio to Writing activities, and block pasting from outside the activity; and in August 2026 Reading Fluency became a subject, with feedback written for a student reading a passage aloud. For a Spanish-speaking student the multilingual settings do the rest: a student may answer in Spanish while you replay it with English captions, you can set the activity to World Language → English so the response has to be in English, and the student can read the AI feedback in Spanish and switch it back to English at any time (40 feedback languages). The library has a ready-made "English Language Development" set. Free Teacher plan: unlimited classes, co-teachers, AI feedback and the library, capped at 20 activities (archive old ones as you go); school plans are quoted. Students join from a link or class code with a Google or Microsoft account, or as a guest if you enable that per activity. Snorkl carries iKeepSafe COPPA and FERPA badges and a Common Sense Privacy seal, strips names before anything goes to the AI provider, shows no ads and sells no data; it will sign a district privacy agreement on request. Recordings need a working mic and browser permission, and the AI sometimes mishears — leave feedback on teacher-approve for the first week and read the captions before you trust a score.

Emerging lane · 90-minute block (Chromebooks, mics on): build one Whiteboard activity the night before: an embedded two-minute science clip plus an Audio Clip of you asking, slowly, "What happens first, next and last? Use because once." Model one recorded answer on the projector, including how to open the AI feedback in Spanish, then switch it to English (10 min). Students watch the clip twice, draw three boxes on the whiteboard, and record a 60-second spoken answer; they read the feedback, then re-record once (25 min). Second activity, subject Reading Fluency: the week's 100-word passage on the whiteboard, read aloud once cold and once after listening to your Audio Clip of the same passage (20 min). Third, a Writing activity with pasting blocked: five sentences about the clip using the three words you pinned, with Snorkl's comments as the revision checklist (20 min). Finish by playing two anonymous responses to the class (peer names hidden in the collaboration setting) and asking what each speaker did well (15 min). Newcomer lane answers the first activity in Spanish first — you read the English captions — then re-records in English from a sentence frame. Challenge lane records a two-minute explanation with one piece of evidence and sets the activity to require English.

4

Ask YouTube · Gemini chat about the video you're watching

Google / YouTube · desktop web Jul 2026, free for all US users Aug 2026, phones Oct 2026 · free, no Premium · browser / Chromebook · 13+, signed in

Under most videos on youtube.com there is now an Ask button (a small sparkle icon). It opens a Gemini side panel that answers questions about that video only: "Explain 0:00–1:30 in simple English," "Quiz me, one question at a time," "I heard harvest — what does it mean here?" or "Here is what I wrote down for the first minute; what did I miss?" For an EL student that turns any clip into a listening check with instant, private feedback, with no new site, account or login beyond YouTube. Brent Warner laid out four classroom routines for it in TESOL's blog (Jan 2026) — fill the gap, quiz me, dictation, vocabulary inference — and Larry Ferlazzo flagged it for ELLs in March 2026. Until August it needed YouTube Premium; it is now free for every signed-in US user 13 and up, on the desktop site (Chromebook) as well as phones and TVs. Privacy per Google's help page: chats are deleted after 45 days and not used for ads; it is off on supervised kid accounts, and it shows up only on "select videos" (most education channels have it). Two things to check before Monday: your district may filter YouTube on student Chromebooks, and Google has not said whether the Ask panel appears on under-18 Workspace for Education accounts — try it on one student Chromebook first. If it is missing for students, run the same routines from your teacher account on the projector, with students writing their answers before you type them into Ask.

Progressing lane · 90-minute block (Chromebooks, captions OFF): pick one 3–5 minute science or news clip. Model the Ask panel on the projector for 10 minutes: ask it to summarize the first minute in simple English, then ask one "what did I miss?" question. Pairs then watch 0:00–1:30, write a three-sentence paraphrase in a Google Doc, paste it into Ask with "what did I leave out or get wrong?" and fix the paraphrase (25 min). Individually, each student runs "Quiz me on this video, 8 questions, one at a time, simple English" and keeps a tally of right answers (20 min). Dictation round: replay 1:30–2:30, students type three sentences they hear, then ask Ask to compare them to the real words (15 min). Exit ticket in Classroom: three words they inferred from the video, each with Ask's one-line explanation and their own sentence (20 min). Challenge lane asks for the quiz "at an advanced level with academic vocabulary" and writes a 100-word summary instead of the tally. Newcomer lane does the block from the projector only, on a Spanish-captioned clip, answering in the chat as a class.

English, no kidding · AI speaking games run from the projector

English, no kidding (Prof. Vito Schiuma) · reviewed by Larry Ferlazzo Jul 2026 · Writing + Google Classroom sync Jul 2026 · free plan: all 26 apps, ~80 AI activities a month · browser, one shared screen

Twenty-six AI-generated speaking and language games that you run from your teacher account on the projector: pick an app and a CEFR level (A1–C2) and it writes the round on the spot — Dialogue Factory (role-plays with a grammar focus you pin), Act it Out, Spin & Speak (one fresh prompt at a time), Definition Wizards (describe a word without the forbidden words), New Word Order (unscramble a sentence, race to the board), Spelling Beef PRO and Number Ninja PRO (hear it, then spell or write it), plus debate formats like Class Clash and Persuasion Arena. Students answer out loud to the room, not on a device, so there are no student accounts and no student data for the speaking games; only you sign in (Google). The free plan has every app and about 80 AI activities a month with no card. The Writing companion (AI feedback, grade push-back to Google Classroom, documented July 2026) and the Listening platform sit on the €15/month plan; Reading is still "coming." Italian company under GDPR, AI runs on Google Gemini; there is no COPPA statement, so keep students off the paid Writing side unless your district clears it.

Emerging lane · 90-minute block (projector, no Chromebooks needed): warm up with Spelling Beef PRO at A1–A2 on the week's ten words (10 min). Run New Word Order at A2 as a race-to-the-board in two teams (15 min). Dialogue Factory at A2 with "past simple" pinned: pairs read the generated dialogue, then perform it with one line changed (25 min). Definition Wizards at A2–B1 for the same ten words (15 min). Finish with each student writing five sentences in a Google Doc using the words they defined (25 min). Progressing lane runs the same block at B1 and swaps the last step for a Class Clash mini-debate.

6

Canva Learn Grid · AI activities in Canva for Education

Canva · new Apr 2026, expanded Oct 2026 · free with Canva for Education (K–12) · browser / Chromebook · FERPA- and COPPA-compliant

Learn Grid is the new learning home inside Canva: 50,000+ curriculum-mapped resources plus an AI generator that turns a topic or a pasted text into an interactive activity in 30+ formats — sorting games, quizzes, whiteboards, graphic organizers, worksheets — in 16+ languages. You adapt one activity up or down ("simplify for Level 2," "add a Spanish–English word bank"), assign it from Canva's Classroom page or run it live, and every student response flows back to you automatically. For World Teachers' Day (October 2026) Canva added email-less student logins: type a list of names and it issues a username and temporary password for each, so a newcomer without a working district email can join the same day. Canva Education is free for K–12 and school-managed. Learn Grid is rolling out by region, so if your Canva shows only the templates library, ask your district's Canva admin. Canva has not published the language list — test the Spanish output before you rely on it.

Emerging lane · 90-minute block: paste a 300-word science text into Learn Grid and generate (1) a Level 2 version with a bilingual word bank, (2) a vocabulary sorting game, and (3) a six-question quiz. Students read the leveled text with a partner (20 min), play the sort twice (15 min), take the quiz (15 min), then write five sentences using the sorted words (25 min). Run the same quiz live for a Progressing group and have each student explain one wrong answer out loud.

Gemini in Google Classroom · student study tools

Google · new Aug 2026 · free with every Workspace for Education edition · admin must enable for students

As of August 10, 2026 the Gemini tab in Classroom is open to students of all ages, not just 18+. A student hovers an assignment under "Due Soon," clicks "Learn with Gemini," and gets starter prompts built from that assignment's own text: make flashcards, quiz me, create a study guide, help me learn. With Gemini Notebook turned on, the same materials become a podcast-style audio overview or a visual diagram. It works in any Classroom-supported language where Gemini is available, so a Spanish-dominant student can ask for Spanish glosses. Your admin has to turn on Gemini in Classroom plus the Gemini app or Gemini Notebook for the student OU; nothing to install on the Chromebook.

Progressing lane · 90-minute block: post a 500-word leveled article as assignment material. Students (1) make ten flashcards with a Spanish gloss on each, (2) run "quiz me" and answer five questions in full sentences, (3) listen to the audio overview once with the text and once without, then (4) write a five-sentence summary in the assignment. Challenge lane adds "explain two things the audio left out."

Read Along in Google Classroom

Google · new June 2026 · free for every Workspace for Education school

Students read aloud to an AI "reading buddy" that gives real-time pronunciation help and asks comprehension questions, with Spanish home-language support on select titles. You see accuracy, fluency and comprehension per student. Needs a mic and a school Google account; admin may need to enable it on older domains.

Newcomer lane · 20-minute station: assign a Lexile-filtered text with Spanish support on. Student reads it aloud twice, then records a 30-second retell.

9

SpeakingMonkey

Independent · surfaced Sept 2026 · free · no account needed

Word-by-word pronunciation scoring against a native-speaker model, with A1–C2 sentence sets in 33 languages. Because there is no login it collects no student data, but it also has no formal student-privacy agreement — treat it as 13+ and teacher-supervised.

Emerging lane · 15-minute "sound of the week": students score five target sentences (/ʃ/ vs /tʃ/, final -ed), screenshot their best score into a Google Doc, then use the sentences in a partner dialogue.
Open SpeakingMonkey → Speaking · Listening
10

MinuteMic

Independent · Aug 2026 · free (Topic of the Day + two practice rounds daily)

Generates a topic, records 60 seconds of speech, and returns feedback on vocabulary, fluency, relevance and structure. Audio is discarded; only the transcript is analyzed. Optional sign-in; assume 13+.

Progressing → Challenge · ELPA21 speaking warm-up: one round cold, read the feedback, outline with a sentence frame, re-record. Challenge lane turns the transcript into a written paragraph.
Open MinuteMic → Speaking · Writing
11

Brisk Boost

Brisk Teaching · updated Sept 2026 · free teacher tier · COPPA-compliant

You build a Tutor, Writing Coach or Debate Partner activity once; students work through it in 50+ languages while you watch in real time, with safety flags and a pause button. The Aug 2026 update generates multi-level and multi-language versions of one resource in a single pass.

Emerging → Progressing: generate one text at Level 2 and Level 4 plus a Spanish version, then assign a Writing Coach that gives sentence-level feedback on a five-sentence response.
