Five decisions, one principle

A counselor makes five decisions for every English learner, and they are all the same decision underneath: place by what the student knows, support by how much English they have. Language level sets the scaffolds, never the ceiling.

1

What do the home-country credits count for?

Mississippi lets a district award credit for coursework completed outside the United States; the district's policy and your evaluation decide what each course equals. The desk below walks the transcript line by line and prints the evaluation.

2

Which courses, this semester?

Grade-level core courses with EL support — not a study hall and not three electives while "English comes." Math and CTE are where newcomers most often show what they already know.

Placement by level →
3

Which diploma path, which tests?

Everyone is on the Traditional diploma (24 credits); endorsements add credits and benchmarks. Four end-of-course tests, each with options when a score is missed, and accommodations that must be in the LSP before the window.

Diplomas, tests, the Seal →
4

Honors, AP, dual credit — yes or no?

Yes where the content is strong. Excluding a student from honors, AP, CTE, athletics or clubs for language reasons is the thing federal reviewers look for first. A newcomer in Honors Geometry with an LSP is normal.

5

The seventeen-year-old Level 1

The hardest schedule in the building and the one most often done wrong. A student who enrolls at 17 with a Level 1 and a thin transcript has a real path to a Mississippi diploma if the plan starts today: a credit evaluation that captures everything already earned, a full load of credit-bearing courses from day one (not a year of "getting English first"), summer and credit-recovery terms planned in advance, the CTE endorsement as a motivating target, and the fact — tell the family — that under state law a student may stay enrolled until age 21. Counseling a student toward a GED/HSE because of English is not a plan; it is the absence of one.

Scheduling rules of thumb

  • EL time is in addition to core content, never instead of it.
  • Put ELs in sections where the content teacher has had the four-lane PD, and cluster two or more ELs per section — one student alone gets no partner talk.
  • Same lunch and same electives as the grade; isolation is a schedule decision.
  • A newcomer's first semester: EL block + math + a hands-on science or CTE + PE/arts, then add social studies — but do not let the first semester be the second semester too.
  • Check the LSP before the schedule is final; accommodations have to be deliverable in the sections chosen.

Grades and retention

  • A Level 1 should earn grades for content shown — diagrams, oral answers, the home language — not an automatic F for English, and not a courtesy pass.
  • Retention for language reasons alone is not defensible; the LSP's grading section is where this is written down.
  • Watch the transcript GPA: honors points count, and so does a year of "study skills" that counts for nothing.

Diplomas, tests and the Seal

One diploma, three endorsements

Traditional24 credits — everyone's path
Career & Technical endorsement26 — Traditional + 4 credits in one CTE program, GPA 2.5, test and credential benchmarks
Academic endorsement26 — Traditional + Algebra II, a 4th science and social studies, GPA 2.5 and benchmarks
Distinguished Academic28 — the Academic set plus higher GPA and ACT benchmarks

Students pick an endorsement before 9th grade and may change it. For a newcomer, the Traditional path with the CTE endorsement in view is the plan that most often finishes.

The four state tests

Algebra I, Biology I, English II, U.S. History — each taken at the end of the course, each required for graduation with options when the score is missed (retakes, course grade combined with other measures). Tell the family: a failed test by itself never means no diploma; the counselor walks the options.

Accommodations on these tests come from the Language Service Plan, must be in place before the window and in routine use in class. A print word-to-word dictionary and extended time are the two most newcomers need.

The ELPT and exit

Every EL takes the ELPT each spring. Exit is Level 4 or higher in all four domains on the same test (State Board, June 2025); after exit the school monitors four years. The counselor is part of that monitoring — a monitored student whose grades slide gets a conversation, not a code change.

The Seal of Biliteracy

Mississippi places a seal on the diploma of a student who shows proficiency in English and a second language by assessments on the state's list. Most Spanish-speaking newcomers already qualify on the Spanish side; the English side arrives with the ACT or state ELA benchmark. Put it on the four-year plan in 9th grade — it is a credential employers and colleges read, and it tells the student the language they brought is an asset. Check MDE's current criteria each year.

Three conversations

To a teacher who wants an EL moved out of honors

"The level tells us how much English the student has, not how much math. The LSP says what supports go with the student — extended time, a word-to-word dictionary, frames for written explanations. Let's look at the first two assessments scored for content and see what they show. If the content isn't there, we'll move; if the English isn't there, we'll scaffold. Removing a student for language reasons is the one thing we can't do."

To a family at the scheduling meeting

English: Here is your child's schedule. These classes earn credit toward graduation. This class is English for new speakers; these are the same classes every student in the grade takes. Your child will have help in each one. Here is the name of the English teacher and how to reach me.

Español: Aquí está el horario de su hijo/a. Estas clases dan créditos para la graduación. Esta clase es inglés para nuevos hablantes; estas son las mismas clases que toman todos los estudiantes del grado. Su hijo/a tendrá apoyo en cada una. Aquí está el nombre de la maestra de inglés y cómo comunicarse conmigo.

To a student who says "I just want to work"

"You can do both, and the diploma makes the work pay better. Let's look at the CTE programs — welding, health science, automotive, culinary, IT — and put you in one this semester. You'll earn a credential and credits at the same time, and your Spanish goes on the diploma as a seal. Four years, and we check the plan every semester."
