The three questions

Every content teacher asks these, usually in this order.

1

There's an EL in my third block on Monday. What do I actually do?

Three moves, about two minutes a lesson: post a language goal beside your content objective, hand over one sentence frame, and let them talk before they write. That is most of the gap, and none of it costs you content time.

150 sentence frames, by level → Paste your lesson, get four lanes back → A newcomer arrived today →
2

How do I grade them without either lying or failing them?

Grade the content. Did the student show the concept — in words, a diagram, a label, or out loud? Give language feedback separately, on one pattern at a time. A correct science answer that loses points for grammar stops telling the truth about science.

What a Level 2 can actually do → Content and language objectives on one page →
3

Am I allowed to change the test?

You are required to provide what is written in the student's Language Service Plan — read-aloud, extra time, a word-to-word glossary. Those are not favours. What you may do on a state test is narrower than what you may do in your own classroom, and the cards below say which is which.

By subject

The same moves land differently depending on what you teach. For your grade band as well as your subject — sixteen squares, each with the language demand, two moves and what to reach for — go to By Subject & Grade.

ScienceLabs are the gift here — the procedure carries the meaning when the words do not. Pre-teach three words, not thirty, and let the diagram be an acceptable answer. EL Bridge will build a four-lane version from the Mississippi standard.
MathThe arithmetic is rarely the problem; the word problem is. Teach the language of the question — best, most likely, except — and let students show the work before they explain it in a sentence.
HistoryThe highest background-knowledge load in the building. Two minutes with one image before the reading fixes more than a vocabulary list does, and your students have their own countries' migrations and elections to connect it to.
ELAYou are already teaching language, so the move is naming it: separate what is a reading problem from what is an English problem, and keep the grade-level text while changing the path to it.

Why this page exists

Most ELs spend most of the day with you, not with me

An English learner at this school gets one period with an EL specialist and six with content teachers. Whatever happens to their English happens mostly in your room, which is unfair to you and true anyway. None of this asks you to become a language teacher — it asks for about two minutes a lesson.
