What Title III can and cannot carry
Required by law regardless of Title III
  • Identification and screening
  • A language instruction program with qualified teachers
  • Core EL staffing and materials

Title VI, EEOA, Castañeda. These come from state and local funds; Title III may not supplant them.

Title III pays for what's added on top
  • Required (c)(1): making the LIEP more effective: supplemental tutoring, newcomer supports, software, curriculum
  • Required (c)(2): PD for EL, content teachers, principals, paras
  • Required (c)(3): parent, family, community engagement
  • Authorized (d): extended day/summer, bilingual aides, dual language, family literacy, supplemental assessments

ESSA §3115(c)–(d). Admin ≤ 2% (§3115(b)). Supplement not supplant (§3115(g)).

Supplement-not-supplant test

Three questions a monitor asks

  1. Would this exist without Title III? If the district paid for it last year from state or local funds, Title III cannot pick it up now.
  2. Is it required by another law? If Title VI or the EEOA requires it (an interpreter at an IEP meeting, the core EL teacher), it is not supplemental.
  3. Do all students get it? If every student receives it, it is not an EL-specific supplement.
Time and effort

Anyone paid in part from Title III keeps monthly personnel activity reports; anyone paid 100% signs a semi-annual certification (2 CFR §200.430). Keep them with the budget.

If Title III disappears

The House FY2027 bill eliminates Title III; the Senate has not acted. This is the contingency view: what you would owe anyway, and where it would come from.

Where to look

Title I, Part A (ELs are eligible; schoolwide plans may fund EL supports); Title II-A for PD if it survives; Title IV-A; state MAEP/general fund; ARP-era carryover if any remains. Document the switch in the plan.
