Claim · evidence · reasoning

Most science writing in grades 6 to 12 has this shape. English learners usually manage the claim and the evidence. The reasoning sentence, the one that says why the evidence supports the claim, is the hard one, so that is the one to model.

Claim: answer the question

Newcomer___ is faster. / The ___ went up.
EmergingI think ___ because I saw ___.
ProgressingWhen ___ increases, ___ increases / decreases.
Challenge___ causes ___, but only when ___.

Evidence: the data, with numbers and units

NewcomerPoints to the row in the table. Copies: "___ = ___ seconds."
EmergingIn the table, ___ was ___ and ___ was ___.
ProgressingThe data show that at ___, the ___ was ___, compared with ___ at ___.
ChallengeAcross all trials, ___ ranged from ___ to ___. The one exception was ___.

Reasoning: why the evidence supports the claim

NewcomerMatches a picture of the idea to the result, with a partner who shares the home language.
EmergingThis happens because ___.
ProgressingThis evidence supports my claim because ___. The science idea is ___.
ChallengeThis is explained by ___. A limitation of the evidence is ___, so a better test would ___.
Model the reasoning sentence on a different lab. Write one full claim, evidence and reasoning on the board from last week's lab, underline the reasoning, and leave it up. Students need to see what "because" is supposed to connect.

Twenty words students think they know

These are the dangerous ones. The student has an everyday meaning, uses it with confidence, and gets the science wrong. Teach both meanings side by side.

A routine for any new term

  1. See it first. The object, a picture, a ten-second demonstration.
  2. Say it and split it. "Photo-synthesis. Light, putting together."
  3. Twin it. Give the Spanish if it is close. Ask other students for the word in their language.
  4. Example and non-example. "This is a solution. This is not. Why?"
  5. Use it today. One sentence aloud to a partner, one in the notebook, with a labeled sketch.

Lab safety, in English and Spanish

Twelve lines for the science teacher who speaks no Spanish. Tap ▶ to hear each one. Show the action while you say it, and have the student show it back before the lab starts.

EnglishEspañol
Put on your goggles. Pónganse las gafas de seguridad.
Tie back long hair. Recójanse el cabello largo.
Do not touch this. It is hot. No toquen esto. Está caliente.
Do not taste or smell anything. No prueben ni huelan nada.
Tell me right away if something spills or breaks. Avísenme de inmediato si algo se derrama o se rompe.
Wash your hands when you finish. Lávense las manos al terminar.
Where is the eyewash station? Show me. ¿Dónde está la estación de lavado de ojos? Muéstrenme.
Stop. Everyone look at me. Alto. Todos mírenme.
Pour slowly. Viertan despacio.
Only do the steps on the page. Hagan solo los pasos que están en la hoja.
Broken glass goes in this box, not the trash. El vidrio roto va en esta caja, no en la basura.
Are you okay? Show me where it hurts. ¿Estás bien? Muéstrame dónde te duele.
About the voices. The audio is your device's built-in synthesized voice, not a recording. These lines support your safety contract; they do not replace it. For other languages, pair the student with a trusted bilingual adult for the safety briefing, and keep picture signs at every station.

Fair assessment in science

Grade the science. Report the English to the EL teacher.

Ways to show the same understanding

  • A labeled diagram with arrows and short phrases
  • A data table and a graph with a one-sentence conclusion
  • Sorting or sequencing cards, then explaining the order aloud
  • An oral explanation at the lab bench, scored on your rubric
  • A report written with the frame for the student's lane

Keep the same

  • The standard and the science idea
  • The data the student has to use
  • The expectation of a claim supported by evidence
  • The key terms. Teach them; do not remove them.
Two scores on the lab report. One for the science: is the claim right, is the evidence from the data, does the reasoning use the idea? One note on the language for the EL teacher. A correct explanation in rough English earns the science points.
