      Read it. Break it. Make it.

      The juicy sentence method in three moves. One hard sentence from today’s reading, ten minutes.

      1

      Read it

      Read the sentence out loud together. Twice.

      2

      Break it

      Ask about one piece at a time:

      Who or what? Does what? When? Why? How?
      3

      Make it

      Students say it in their own words. Then they write one like it.

      One rule: use the real sentence. Don’t make it easier first.

      ● Emerging: give the pieces already cut. One-word answers are fine.   ◆ Progressing: students find the pieces and answer in full sentences.

      A simplified version of the juicy sentence work of Lily Wong Fillmore and Charles Fillmore (Stanford, 2012). EL Publishing.

      Ten minutes, one sentence

      Juicy Sentence Studio

      One hard sentence a week for each course: Biology I, U.S. History, Algebra I and English II, 36 weeks each and in course order. Find the core, chunk it, say it simply in English and Spanish, then write one like it. Every week also becomes a ready-made Monday Move for the whole building.

      The routine (same every week)
      1. Read it twice, out loud (1 min)
      2. Find the core: who or what does what? (2 min)
      3. Chunk it and question each chunk (3 min)
      4. Say it simply, in English and Spanish (2 min)
      5. Write one like it (2 min)

      A PLC series in 12 sessions

      The Teacher Writes Too

      We ask students to read and write in every subject, but we rarely do it ourselves under the same conditions. In each 30-minute session, teachers read a short model, write in their own subject under one constraint, and feel what that constraint costs. Then they take one move back to class. Sessions 9–12 turn to responding to EL writing.
