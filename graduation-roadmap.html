1 · Pick a diploma · Elige un diploma

Mississippi offers one diploma with optional endorsements. Everyone starts on the Traditional path (24 credits); endorsements add credits, a GPA floor and test benchmarks. The state asks students to pick an endorsement before 9th grade, but you can change it — pick what fits now.

Newcomer note: a student can attend public high school until age 21 in Mississippi, so a 5-year plan is allowed and often wise for students who arrive at 16–17 with little English. Ask the counselor about it in 9th or 10th grade, not senior year. Se puede asistir hasta los 21 años; un plan de 5 años es posible — pregunta temprano.

3 · State tests · Exámenes estatales

Mississippi requires an end-of-course test in four classes. Check them off as you pass. If a test is failed, there are alternate routes (ACT scores, retakes, course-grade combinations) — the counselor will explain them; a failed test never means "no diploma" by itself. EL students may receive accommodations such as extra time or a word-to-word dictionary.

4 · Classes from your home country · Clases de tu país

  • Bring every document: report cards, certificates, "boleta," "constancia," "libreta" — in any language, even photos on a phone.
  • The school evaluates them and can award credit for classes you passed: math, science, social studies, and your first language counts as foreign-language credit.
  • Grades 7–8 (secundaria in Mexico, básica in Central America) usually map to U.S. middle school; grade 9+ (preparatoria / bachillerato / diversificado) can map to high school credits.
  • No documents? You can still be placed by age and tested for credit in some subjects. Ask.
  • Ask for the evaluation in writing and keep a copy. If a credit seems missing, ask the counselor to review it with the EL teacher.
  • Trae todos los documentos: boletas, certificados, constancias, libretas — en cualquier idioma, incluso fotos en el teléfono.
  • La escuela los evalúa y puede dar crédito por las clases aprobadas: matemáticas, ciencias, estudios sociales, y tu primer idioma cuenta como crédito de lengua extranjera.
  • Los grados 7–8 (secundaria en México, básica en Centroamérica) suelen corresponder a middle school; el grado 9 en adelante (preparatoria / bachillerato / diversificado) puede dar créditos de high school.
  • ¿Sin documentos? Aun así te ubican por edad y en algunas materias puedes hacer un examen para obtener crédito. Pregunta.
  • Pide la evaluación por escrito y guarda una copia. Si falta un crédito, pide al consejero que lo revise con el maestro de EL.
Seal of Biliteracy · Sello de Alfabetización Bilingüe: Mississippi puts a seal on the diploma of students who show proficiency in English and another language. English counts with an ELPT score of 4 in all domains, English II test score of 4, ACT English 18, SAT 320 or AP English 3; the other language with AP (3), AAPPL or STAMP Intermediate-Mid (I4/L4) in all four skills, or a portfolio for languages without a test. Your home language is an asset — ask the counselor in 10th or 11th grade.

5 · My four-year plan · Mi plan de cuatro años

Write the classes for each year (your counselor can help). Keep ESL/ELD on the list every year you need it — it counts as credit. A typical year is 7–8 credits.

9th · FreshmanGoal: pass Algebra I & Biology I tests. Join one activity.
10th · SophomoreGoal: pass English II test. Talk about a 4- or 5-year plan.
11th · JuniorGoal: pass U.S. History test. Take the ACT (free at school). Ask about the Seal of Biliteracy.
12th · SeniorGoal: FAFSA or state aid form, college / job applications, final credit check in September.
